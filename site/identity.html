Identity · on behalf of

An agent should not hold the person's keys.

When a person asks Maestro a question, the domain agents answer for that person. They need enough permission to do their one job, for as long as the job takes, and no more. This page explains how the lab does that, without assuming you know anything about authentication.

The problem in one picture

A token is a visitor badge

A token is a signed note from the identity provider: who you are, which doors you may open, until when. Hand an agent the person's own badge and you give it every door the person has, for the whole day, with no trace of who carried it.

Same person. Narrower badge. That narrowing is called delegation: the agent acts on behalf of the person, with a subset of the person's rights, and the badge says so.

What Keycloak actually issued

These are the claims of real tokens, trimmed. The exchange is the OAuth 2.0 token exchange standard (RFC 8693). Microsoft Entra calls the same pattern the on-behalf-of flow, which is where the name comes from.

The caller's badge (subject token)

Obtained by the IDE (password grant on ide-cli) or by the console (client credentials). Note aud includes maestro: Keycloak only exchanges a token that names the requester.

Why not…

The alternatives, and why they lost

…give Maestro its own badge?

Then every agent sees "Maestro asked" and nothing else: no person, so no way to limit Maestro to what this person may do. Every caller would inherit Maestro's full reach. That is the classic confused deputy. Maestro's own identity is used for exactly one thing: proving to Keycloak that it is Maestro asking for the exchange.

…just pass the person's badge through?

It works, and it was the lab's first design. But the agent holds every door for hours, and nothing says who carried the badge. Kept as a switchable comparison mode, not as the default.

…let Maestro pretend to be the person?

Impersonation (a new badge with no act) is indistinguishable from the person acting alone. The whole point is the chain of custody. Rejected.

…build a second identity provider?

In an enterprise the person's badge comes from the corporate IdP (Entra, Okta) and the agent platform has a realm that trusts it. We considered building that here. But the narrowing happens inside the platform realm either way, because that is the only place that knows the agents exist. One realm shows it truthfully; the enterprise mapping is one brokering link away.

Glossary

The words on the badges

token / JWT
The badge: a signed JSON note from Keycloak. Anyone can read it; nobody can forge it.
sub
Who the badge is for. Stays the person through the whole chain.
azp
Which application the badge was issued to: ide-cli for the person's own badge, maestro for the on-behalf-of badge.
act
"Actor": who is carrying the badge on the person's behalf. Present only on delegated badges.
aud
"Audience": who the badge is addressed to. An agent refuses badges addressed to someone else.
realm_access.roles
The doors: domain:cache means "may consult the cache agent". The gateway reads this.
scope
What Maestro asked for in the exchange; domain-cache maps to the one role and the one audience.
token exchange
RFC 8693: trade one badge for a narrower one, authenticated as the carrier. Keycloak calls it standard token exchange.
